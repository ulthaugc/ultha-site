How Salesloft turned customer reviews into $1.8M in pipeline

They had thousands of users who liked the product. Things changed when they started asking them on purpose, and handed the answers to sales.

Ultha Moktan · Oct 2026 · 6 min read

I didn't work with Salesloft. This is a breakdown of their published case study, so you can see what happens when someone treats customer proof like a system instead of a one-off ask.

The short version
  • Salesloft had been on G2 since 2014, but nobody owned reviews until 2018.
  • They started asking on a schedule, thanked every reviewer, answered every review and put the best quotes in their sales team's hands.
  • Reported result: $1.82M in pipeline and $781K in revenue tied to G2, with an 85% response rate on review asks.

Happy customers, quiet proof

Salesloft makes sales software and had plenty of users who liked it. They'd had a G2 profile for years. What they didn't have was a plan. Reviews came in when they came in.

That changed in 2018, when Alexandria Snow joined as product marketing director and decided customer voice should be part of how they sell, not just a badge on the website.

What they did

  1. Asked on a schedule

    They picked customers likely to review, and G2 sent a planned series of emails linking to Salesloft's review page. Snow added personal emails on top.

  2. Thanked everyone, whatever they said

    Reviewers got a gift card as a thank-you, no matter what they wrote. That keeps reviews honest, which is exactly why buyers trust them.

  3. Put reviews in sales' hands

    They turned reviews into a "Customer Story Flipbook," used them in sales decks and featured them on their website. Snow called the flipbook one of their most popular sales tools.

  4. Answered every review, even the bad ones

    The team replied to reviews and worked directly with unhappy customers to fix problems. A good reply to a bad review can do more than another 5-star one.

And Snow said those numbers undersell it, because G2 had a touchpoint in essentially every deal. Reviews weren't a marketing side project. They showed up in sales conversations everywhere.

Why this matters more now

In 2022, reviews helped buyers who visited G2. In 2026, AI reads them for everyone.

  • 51% of software buyers now start research in an AI chatbot more often than Google. A year earlier it was 29%.
  • Buyers say citations from review sites are their top trust signal when AI recommends a vendor.
  • AI tools lean on recent reviews. A pile of reviews from years ago fades, so the reviews you collect this quarter are the ones AI reads next quarter.
Source: G2, survey of 1,076 B2B software buyers, March 2026.

What this looks like for a startup

You don't need 2,600 reviews or a product marketing team. You need the same four habits, sized for a team where the founder still answers support tickets.

Quick answers

Did Salesloft pay for reviews?

They gave reviewers a gift card as a thank-you, and it was given no matter what the review said. That's different from paying for positive reviews, which review sites don't allow.

How many reviews does a startup need?

There's no magic number. Start with your happiest customers and keep reviews coming every month. Recent, detailed reviews count for more than a big old pile.

Why do reviews matter for AI search?

AI tools pull from sites that aren't yours, like review sites, when they answer 'what's the best tool for X?' In G2's 2026 survey, buyers named review-site citations as their top trust signal in AI answers.
