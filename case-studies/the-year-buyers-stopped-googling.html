The year buyers stopped Googling software first

In twelve months, software buyers moved their research from Google to AI chatbots. Here is what changed, and what it means if AI doesn't know your name.

Ultha Moktan · Oct 2026 · 6 min read

This one is a market case study. Instead of one company, it follows what happened to software buying in a single year, using big buyer surveys from G2, Gartner and Forrester.

The short version
  • In March 2026, 51% of software buyers started research in an AI chatbot more often than Google. A year earlier it was 29%.
  • 69% ended up picking a different vendor than they planned because of a chatbot's answer. 1 in 3 bought from a vendor they'd never heard of.
  • AI answers lean on reviews and real customer stories. If yours are thin, you're less likely to be named, and you may never know you lost the deal.

What changed in one year

Most buying shifts take a decade. This one took about twelve months. Here's the same question asked a year apart.

And it isn't a niche habit. In Forrester's 2024 survey, 89% of B2B buyers had already used generative AI in at least one part of their buying process.

The deals you never hear about

Here's the part that should worry any founder. When buyers ask AI for recommendations, they act on the answer.

#1AI chatbots became the top source shaping vendor shortlists, just ahead of review sitesG2, 2025 Buyer Behavior Report

Put that next to Gartner's finding that buyers spend only 17% of their buying time talking to vendors at all. The shortlist gets written in the other 83%, and more and more of that is a conversation with AI. If you're not in the answer, you're often out before anyone books a call.

Where AI gets its answers

AI tools don't take your word for it. They look for what other people say about you.

  • Review sites carry weight. Buyers say citations from software review sites are their top trust signal in AI answers.
  • Thin proof makes AI hesitate. A weak review presence makes AI less confident, and less likely to recommend you.
  • Fresh beats old. Marketers are now prioritizing review recency and volume, because models increasingly favor recent reviews.
  • Consistency builds trust. Buyers see it as a good sign when ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude all describe a vendor the same way.
Why waiting is expensive

The shift happened during this buying cycle, not some future one.

  • Buyers who start in a chatbot form a shortlist before they ever visit your site.
  • Proof takes time to build up. Every quarter you wait is a quarter of reviews and stories your competitors collect and you don't.
  • With 1 in 3 buyers picking a vendor they'd never heard of, being unknown isn't safe anymore. Someone else is happy to be the name AI suggests.
Sources: G2 (March 2026), Gartner, Forrester.

Quick answers

Is Google still worth it for B2B software?

Yes. In G2's 2026 survey, 61% of buyers used AI search alongside Google. The point isn't to drop Google. It's to make sure that when buyers ask AI, you're part of the answer too.

How do I get my startup mentioned by ChatGPT?

Give it real things to read about you on sites you don't own: recent reviews, customer stories and comparisons. AI tools trust what customers say about you more than what you say about yourself.

Do these numbers apply to startups or just big companies?

The surveys cover B2B software buyers across company sizes. The habit of asking AI first applies no matter who you're selling to, and smaller vendors gain the most when buyers are open to names they've never heard of.
