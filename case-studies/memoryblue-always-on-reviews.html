memoryBlue: $800K in pipeline from a steady review habit

Not one big push. Small, steady asks at the moments customers were already happy, kept up quarter after quarter.

Ultha Niraula · Oct 2026 · 5 min read

I didn't work with memoryBlue. This breakdown comes from their published G2 case study. It's a good example of the slow, steady version of customer proof, the kind you can't rush later.

The short version
  • memoryBlue, a B2B sales development firm, asks for reviews at customer milestones and alongside its quarterly customer survey.
  • It always has a review campaign running, and its delivery teams compete on a leaderboard.
  • Reported result: 300+ verified reviews, Leader status in two categories for 8 quarters in a row, and $800K in influenced pipeline tracked in a single month.

Not one big push

A lot of companies treat reviews like spring cleaning: one big burst, then nothing for a year. memoryBlue went the other way. They built small asks into moments that were already happening.

The habit, in four parts

  1. Ask at the happy moments

    Review requests go out at customer milestones, when results are fresh and the customer is most likely to say yes.

  2. Ride along with the survey

    Asks are synced with the quarterly customer satisfaction survey, so customers who just gave a high score get an easy next step.

  3. Make it a team sport

    Delivery teams compete on a review leaderboard. The people closest to customers own the ask, not just marketing.

  4. Never switch it off

    At least one review campaign is always running, and G2 is connected to their CRM so they can see which deals reviews touched.

Why the timing matters

Proof compounds. The clock starts the day you start.

  • Buyers spend only 17% of their buying time talking to vendors. The rest is research, and that's where reviews and stories do the selling.
  • For enterprise buyers, review sites are the #1 research source (56%), just ahead of AI search (55%).
  • AI tools increasingly favor recent reviews. A steady habit keeps you fresh. A one-time push goes stale.

Quick answers

When is the best time to ask a customer for a review?

Right after a win: a milestone, a great support moment, or a high score on your satisfaction survey. That's when results are fresh and saying yes feels easy.

Should sales or marketing own reviews?

Whoever is closest to the customer. memoryBlue gave the ask to its delivery teams and made it a friendly competition, which kept reviews coming without a big campaign.

How do I know if reviews are helping sales?

Connect your review platform to your CRM if you can, and ask new customers what they read before buying. memoryBlue tracked $800K in influenced pipeline in one month this way.
