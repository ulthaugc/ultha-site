The exact checklist I use with startups: who to ask, what to send, what to ask on the call, and where to post it so prospects (and AI search) find it.

takes about 2 hours total

1

Pick 5 customers to ask

Start with the people who already say nice things. You're not convincing anyone. You're just writing it down.

  • Customers who've used you for 2+ months
  • Someone who sent a "this is great" message, Slack, or support ticket
  • People who renewed, upgraded or referred someone
  • A mix of roles: a founder, a manager and a daily user
  • Your champion at your best-known logo

Search your inbox and Slack for "love", "amazing", "saved us". That's your list.

2

Ask for a feedback call (not a testimonial)

Don't lead with "can we feature you?" Ask for honest feedback. People love being asked for their opinion, and the best quotes come out naturally once they're talking.

Subject: Quick chat next week? Hi [Name], When you said [their exact words, e.g. "it’s the one tool everyone actually uses"], it stuck with me. We're so glad it's been working for you, and we'd love to learn from you: what's working, and what we could still improve. Do you have 20 minutes for a quick chat next week? Any of these work? • [Tue 10am] • [Wed 2pm] • [Thu 11am] Thank you, [Your name]

Send it from someone they already talk to: their account manager, customer success lead or the founder they met. A name they recognize gets opened. A no-reply address gets ignored.

Use their exact words from a past email, Slack or call. Follow up once after 4 days.

Only at the end of the call, if they've said great things, ask: "Would you be open to us sharing what you just said? I'll send it to you to approve first."

3

On the call: 10 questions

Record it (with permission). Ask, then stay quiet. The best lines come after a pause.

  1. What were you using before us, and what was frustrating about it?
  2. What made you start looking for something new?
  3. What almost stopped you from choosing us?
  4. What did the first week look like?
  5. What's one thing that's easier now?
  6. Can you put a number on it: time, money, headcount, hours?
  7. Who on your team uses it most, and what do they say?
  8. If we disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss?
  9. How would you describe us to a friend in one sentence?
  10. Who else do you know who should be using this?

Questions 6 and 9 give you your headline quote. Question 10 gives you your next lead.

4

Turn one call into three assets

Don't let a great call die in a recording folder.

⭐ A reviewSend them the G2 or Google link right after the call, while they're warm. Suggest 2–3 points they said.
💬 A testimonialOne quote + name, role, company, photo. Use their exact words from Q9 or Q6.
📄 A mini case studyBefore → why they switched → what changed (with a number). 300 words is plenty.

Always send the draft back for approval. It builds trust and they often add a better line.

5

Post it where prospects (and LLMs) look

A great quote on a slide deck helps no one. Put it where people search.

  • G2, Capterra or Google Business: whichever your buyers check
  • Your homepage, near your main button
  • Your pricing page (that's where doubts show up)
  • A dedicated case study page with a clear title
  • LinkedIn: the founder posts the story, tags the customer
  • Reddit or community threads where your category comes up
  • A short video version on YouTube (60 seconds is enough)

AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini lean on reviews and pages you don't own. Third-party sites count more than your own homepage.

6

Make it a weekly habit

Proof compounds. One call a week is 50 stories a year.

  • Book 1 customer call every week
  • Publish 1 asset within 7 days of each call
  • Ask every happy support ticket for a review
  • Once a month, ask a few AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) "what's the best tool for [your category]?" and note if you show up
