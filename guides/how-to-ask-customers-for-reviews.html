How to ask customers for reviews (without it being awkward)

Most customers are happy to leave a review. They just need the right moment, the right person asking and an email that takes 30 seconds to say yes to.

The short version
  • Timing: ask right after a win, not at random.
  • Sender: the person they already talk to, not a no-reply address.
  • Best on a call: ask at the end of a good video call, then send the link.
  • Over email: short, personal, one link, and a reminder of what they said.
  • Follow up once after 4 to 5 days. Still nothing? Bring it up on your next call.

Most review requests look the same. "Dear valued customer, we would appreciate it if…" They go out to the whole list at once, get archived, and the team decides "our customers just don't leave reviews."

They usually do. They just weren't asked well.

The fix isn't a bigger list. It's a better ask.

Why it's worth getting right now

Reviews used to help the buyers who happened to visit G2. Now AI tools read them for everyone.

#1

Citations from software review sites are buyers' top trust signal when an AI chatbot recommends a vendor.G2, survey of 1,076 B2B software buyers, March 2026

And AI tools lean on recent reviews, so a steady trickle beats one big push every couple of years.

💡My tip: Search your inbox and Slack for "love", "amazing" and "saved us". That's your list for this month.

2. Have the right person ask

A request from "noreply@" feels like a survey. A note from someone they know feels like a favor between people who like each other. Send it from their account manager, customer success lead or the founder they met.

3. How to ask for a review over email

🎥The best way is on a video call. At the end of a good call, ask in person: "Would you be open to sharing that in a quick review? I'll send you the link." People rarely say no face to face. Then send the link right after. The emails below are for when a call isn't coming up soon.

Over email, short and personal wins. Three versions, depending on the moment. Copy the one that fits and swap in your customer's name and words.

Email 1 · After a win they told you about
Subject: made our week, thank you!
Hi Jake,

Loved hearing that onboarding went from three weeks to four days. The whole team was so happy to hear it.

Would you be up for sharing that in a quick review? Two or three sentences in your own words is perfect. It really helps teams like yours find us.

[link]

And if now's not a good time, no worries at all.

Ultha
Email 2 · After a thank-you or a great score
Subject: your note made my day
Hi Emily,

Your note yesterday honestly made my day. Thank you for taking the time.

Can I ask a small favor? If you're open to it, a short, honest review would help other founders decide if we're right for them. It takes about two minutes:

[link]

Either way, I'm really glad it's working for you.

Ultha
Email 3 · One gentle follow-up (4 to 5 days later, as a reply)
Reply to your first email
Hi Jake, just bumping this in case it got buried under everything else.

Here's the link again if you have two minutes: [link]

No pressure at all, and thanks either way!
💡My tip: if there's still no reply after the follow-up, stop emailing. Add it to the agenda for your next call and bring it up casually at the end: "Oh, and if you ever get a sec, that review would mean a lot."

Quick answers

When is the best time to ask a customer for a review?

Right after a win: a goal they hit, a thank-you message, a great support moment or a high score on your satisfaction survey. That's when the good experience is fresh.

Who should ask for the review?

Someone the customer already knows, like their account manager, customer success lead or the founder they met. A name they recognize gets opened.

Is it OK to offer a gift card for a review?

A small thank-you is fine if every reviewer gets it no matter what they write, and the review site allows it. Never pay for positive reviews. Check each site's rules first.

How many times should I follow up?

Once. Send one friendly nudge after 4 to 5 days. If there is still no reply, stop emailing and bring it up at the end of your next call instead.
