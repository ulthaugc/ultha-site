Your best sales pitch is buried in a Zoom recording

How to turn one 15-minute customer call into four things that help you sell, without asking your customer for more of their time.

Ultha Niraula · Oct 2026 · 6 min read
The short version
  • Your customer gave you 15 minutes. Don't come back next month for a quote, then a video, then a sign-off.
  • Treat the call as raw material: a short video, social clips, a written story and a one-pager for sales.
  • Always get their OK before anything goes live. Nothing should surprise them.

Somewhere in your Google Drive, there's a recording of a customer saying something amazing about your product.

Nobody has watched it since.

When I worked in product, the one rule in customer research was: protect the relationship. Your customers' time and goodwill are worth more than any single quote.

That's why I think about a customer call differently. It's not one piece of content. It's the raw material for a lot of them.

The 2 mistakes I see founders make

1. They post the whole thing. A 15-minute video goes up on YouTube or the website, and almost nobody finishes it.

~50%

is about how much of a video people watch, even when it's under a minute long. Testimonial videos average 46%.Wistia, video engagement data

2. They ask over and over. A call in January, a written quote in February, a logo approval in March. Each ask spends a little more of your customer's patience. Or worse, the customer finds their face somewhere they never agreed to.

One call, four ways to use it

Tap each one to see how I build it and where it goes.

A 60-second customer story (for your website)

Nobody needs their whole life story on your homepage. They need to see how one workday got better.

10s · the old headache30s · the moment it clicked20s · what changed
📍 Where it goes: the top of your homepage or product page, and your welcome email.

Three 15-second clips (for LinkedIn)

Pick the three best sentences from the call. Add clear captions, since most people scroll with the sound off, and keep your brand colors.

For example: one clip of the customer saying Friday reports went from four hours to twenty minutes, another of them saying the switch took one night.

📍 Where it goes: the founder's LinkedIn and the company page. Keep it to normal posts. Only use their face in paid ads if they say yes to that too.

A written customer story (for your website, and for AI search)

AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity mostly read text. If the best proof you have is in a video, AI tools can't find it or quote it.

So write it up: the problem, the moment it clicked, the result, with their real numbers and quotes. Use clear headings and short lists so it's easy to skim, for people and for AI.

85%

of software buyers think more highly of a company when an AI tool mentions it in an answer.G2, survey of 1,076 B2B buyers, March 2026

📍 Where it goes: a "Customer stories" page on your website.

A one-page summary (for your sales team)

Whoever is selling, even if that's still you, doesn't have time to send a video file. They need something they can drop into an email to move a stuck deal forward.

One page: the customer's logo, the single biggest result ("Saves 12 hours a week"), and a two-sentence quote.

📍 Where it goes: follow-up emails, sales templates and LinkedIn messages to prospects.

The step you can't skip: their OK

This whole thing only works if your customer feels safe. Nobody should scroll LinkedIn and be surprised to see their own face. That's the fastest way to lose a good customer relationship.

Before anything goes live, send them everything in one easy, low-pressure message. Here's the one I use:

To: your customer · Subject: Quick look before anything goes live
Hi [Name],

Thanks again for chatting with me! I pulled together a short video and a few quotes from our conversation.

Do these feel right to you? If you'd like anything changed or removed, just say the word. Nothing goes live without your OK.

Thank you!

Give them full say and make it easy to review, and two things happen. They feel respected. And when the pieces look good, customers often want to share them on their own LinkedIn, which builds trust for you.

You don't need 20 customers

You need a few people who love your product, one good conversation with each, and a respectful way to turn it into the right formats.

Quick answers

What can I make from one customer interview?

A 60-second story video, two or three 15-second clips, a written customer story for your website, and a one-page summary for sales.

How long should a testimonial video be?

Around 60 seconds for your website and about 15 seconds for social. Even videos under a minute keep only about half their viewers, so shorter is safer.

Can AI search tools read video testimonials?

AI tools mostly rely on text. Turn your video quotes and results into a written customer story so AI tools can find and quote it.

Do I need my customer's permission?

Yes. Send every piece before it goes live and let them change or remove anything.
