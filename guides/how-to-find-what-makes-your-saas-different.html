How to find what makes your SaaS different (ask your customers, not your team)

If your homepage could swap logos with a competitor and nobody would notice, this one's for you. Your real difference is already out there, in your customers' words.

Ultha Moktan · Oct 2026 · 6 min read

The short version
  • Most SaaS copy sounds the same because the team writes it, in the team's words.
  • Your customers already know why they picked you over the others. Ask them.
  • Talk to 5 to 8 recent customers, write down their exact words, and look for the reason that keeps repeating. That's your difference.

As a product manager, I sat in a lot of meetings about "what makes us different." We'd fill a whiteboard with features. Faster. Smarter. AI-powered.

Then I'd get on a call with a customer, ask why they picked us, and hear something none of us had written down.

The customer was right every time.

Why you sound like everyone else

Read five homepages in your category. You'll see the same words: AI-powered, all-in-one, streamline your workflow. Nobody's lying. They're just all describing themselves from the inside.

It's getting worse, too. More tools are built on the same models, use the same design styles and write copy with the same AI helpers. From a buyer's side, a lot of products now look alike, a problem First Round recently wrote about for AI startups.

Buyers don't talk like that. They say things like "it's the one my team actually opens" or "setup took an afternoon, not a quarter." That plain, specific language is what makes you stand out.

Where your real difference hides

Not in your feature list. Your difference lives in the moment a customer compared you to the alternatives and picked you. That moment is in:

4 questions that find it

Talk to customers who bought in the last few months. They still remember what else they looked at, and why they picked you.

1 · The shortlist

Find out who you beat

Ask this"When you were looking, what else did you consider? Why did you pick us over those?"
Example answer
"The other two could do the same thing on paper. But yours was the only one my ops lead could set up without IT."
💡My tip: Listen for the reason that isn't a feature. "Without IT" is a difference. "Has integrations" isn't.
2 · The doubt

Ask what almost stopped them

Ask this"What almost made you go with someone else? What changed your mind?"
Example answer
"Honestly, you were pricier. But the trial showed us we'd save a full day every week, so it paid for itself."
💡My tip: The doubt they overcame is the doubt your next buyer has. Answer it on your pricing page.
3 · The aha

Find the moment it clicked

Ask this"Was there a moment early on when you thought, 'Okay, this is it'? What was happening?"
Example answer
"The first Monday, I opened it and the whole week was already planned. I didn't have to chase anyone."
💡My tip: Stay quiet after you ask. The best answers come right after the pause.
4 · The friend test

Hear how they'd pitch you

Ask this"If a friend asked what makes you different from the other tools, what would you say?"
Example answer
"It's the one your team will actually use. That's it."
💡My tip: This answer is often a better headline than the one on your homepage now.

Spot the pattern

After 5 to 8 calls, put every answer in one doc. Highlight the phrases that show up more than once. You're looking for:

If nothing repeats, that's a finding too. It usually means you're selling to a few different kinds of customers, each picking you for their own reason. Group the calls by customer type and look again.

Quick answers

How do I find my SaaS product's differentiator?

Ask 5 to 8 recent customers why they picked you over the other options, what almost stopped them, and how they'd describe you to a friend. Write down their exact words, then look for the phrase that keeps coming back. That repeated phrase is your differentiator.

Why does my SaaS sound like every competitor?

Because most homepage copy is written by the team, in the team's words: features, AI, workflow, all-in-one. Competitors do the same, so everyone ends up sounding alike. Customers describe you in plainer, more specific words.

How many customer interviews do I need to find my positioning?

Five to eight is usually enough to hear the same reason repeat. Talk to customers who bought in the last few months, since they remember why they chose you best.

What do I do once I find my differentiator?

Use their words in your homepage headline, sales deck and review requests, and turn the best quotes into testimonials and case studies so buyers and AI search see the same reason everywhere.
