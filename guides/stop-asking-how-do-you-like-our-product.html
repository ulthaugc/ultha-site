Stop asking customers "How do you like our product?"

It gets you "It's great!" and nothing else. Here are 5 questions that get customers to tell the real story instead.

Ultha Niraula · Oct 2026 · 5 min read
The short version
  • "How do you like our product?" gets a polite "It's great!" That won't help a new buyer decide.
  • Ask about their workday instead: what it looked like before, the moment it clicked, what changed.
  • One 15-minute chat with these 5 questions gives you real quotes for your website, reviews and videos.

The first time I asked a customer "How do you like the product?", she said "It's great!" And that was it.

Worse, that one question set the tone for the whole call. It became her vs. the person from the software company. She was being polite. I was fishing for a compliment.

Not how you want to start a call.

Over years of customer calls as a product manager, I learned something simple: people don't open up when you ask them to rate you. They open up when you ask about their day.

Why "It's great!" doesn't help you sell

Polite praise feels nice. But when a new buyer reads "great tool, great team," they learn nothing. It doesn't answer the question they actually have: will this make my week easier?

And buyers are reading more of what your customers say than ever, often through AI tools.

51%

of software buyers now start their research with an AI chatbot instead of Google. Review sites are the second biggest influence on their shortlist, right after AI.G2, survey of 1,076 B2B buyers, March 2026

AI tools and buyers both look for the same thing: specific, real stories. "Great tool!" isn't one.

The 5 questions to ask instead

Each one swaps a question that gets a polite answer for one that gets a story.

01The before

Skip: “What were you using before us?” → “Google Sheets. It was slow.”

“Before you started using us, what did your day-to-day actually look like? What was the biggest headache?”

“Honestly, I spent every Friday afternoon copying data from one sheet to another, and we kept losing track of client updates. It was a nightmare.”Example answer

💡 Let them vent. The more detail about the old way, the stronger the “after” sounds.

02The aha moment

Skip: “Why did you choose us?” → “Best features for the price.”

“Was there a moment early on when you thought, ‘Okay, this is exactly what we need’? What was happening?”

“The first time the dashboard updated in real time without me refreshing anything, I knew we weren't going back.”Example answer

💡 If they pause, wait. The best answers usually come right after the silence.

03The switch

Skip: “Was setup easy?” → “Yes, your team was helpful.”

“Switching tools is always a bit stressful. How did setup compare to what you expected? Anything that surprised you?”

“I was dreading it. But all our old data moved over in one night and nothing broke. Huge relief.”Example answer

💡 This answer is gold for buyers who are scared to switch. Put it near your pricing page.

04The change

Skip: “What's your ROI?” → “Great ROI and time savings.”

“Looking at things now, what's the biggest difference it's made for your team? Any time saved you've noticed?”

“Our Friday reporting went from four hours to about twenty minutes. Now we actually spend that time with clients.”Example answer

💡 Ask for a number, but don't push. “About 20 minutes” is plenty.

05The friend test

Skip: “Would you recommend us?” → “Yes, highly recommend!”

“If a friend in your industry asked about us over coffee, how would you describe what we do?”

“I'd say: if you're tired of managing chaos and want something that just works in the background, switch.”Example answer

💡 Their answer is often better than your homepage headline. Use it (with their OK).

3 things that make the call work

What to do with the answers

Stop asking stiff questions and you stop getting stiff answers. One call like this gives you real problems, real moments and real numbers, which is everything you need for reviews, testimonials, a short video or a customer story.

The format matters less than the story. But there's a smart way to turn one call into all of them, without asking your customer for more of their time.

Quick answers

What should I ask customers to get a good testimonial?

Ask about their workday, not about your product: what it looked like before, the moment it clicked, how the switch went, what changed, and how they'd describe you to a friend.

How long should a customer interview be?

About 15 to 20 minutes. Five good questions are enough to get real stories, specific results and a quote you can use.

How do I ask for a testimonial without it being awkward?

Call it a feedback chat. At the end, ask "Can I share what you just said?" and send them the quote to approve before it goes anywhere.

Should I record customer calls?

Yes, with permission. You'll listen better and have their exact words to quote later.
